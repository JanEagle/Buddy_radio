For mission makers

Buddy Radio works with any mission, with no triggers or scripts to add. The bridge runs inside DCS and reads the mission as it plays. A few choices in the mission editor make the stations sound better.

Tankers

Frequency and TACAN

Overlord reads out a tanker's radio frequency and TACAN from the mission file. Set them in the editor and pilots get them with every "request tanker".

  • Task: give the group the Refueling task, as every DCS tanker needs.
  • Radio: set the group's frequency in the editor. Pick one the group doesn't use for anything else.
  • TACAN: add an Activate Beacon command on a waypoint, with channel and X or Y. Overlord reads the channel and mode.
  • Callsign: Texaco, Arco and Shell are read as "Texaco 1-1".
  • Boom or basket comes from the type: the KC-135 has a boom; the KC-135 MPRS, KC-130, S-3B tanker and Il-78 have baskets. Put one of each up if your group flies both kinds of jet.

Tankers spawned by scripts

For MOOSE, MIST and missions like Foothold, keep a late-activated template group with these settings. Spawned copies named like Texaco#001 are matched to their template. A frequency or TACAN set by a script while the mission runs can't be seen, so Overlord leaves it out.

Airfields

Towers and FARPs

Tower serves every airfield on the map with no setup. The runway in use, wind, altimeter and ATIS come from the live mission weather.

  • An airfield with its own frequency: on the app's Stations page, choose Add a tower and ATIS and write the airfield name exactly as DCS shows it.
  • FARPs: a helicopter standing on a friendly FARP talks to that FARP.
  • Weather: wind and cloud set in the editor change the runway in use and the ATIS, so a windy mission sounds different from a calm one.
Carriers

For Paddles

Paddles uses the carrier's live position, heading, speed and wind, on any coalition carrier. For good Case I passes, route the carrier into the wind fast enough for about 25 knots over the deck, and give it a long straight leg during recovery.

Threats

What Overlord sees

  • Overlord knows every active enemy unit in the mission. It doesn't model radar detection, so hide surprises with late activation rather than terrain.
  • Late-activated groups appear as soon as they're activated.
  • SAM and triple-A warnings know the engagement range of around 30 types, from the SA-2 and SA-10 down to Shilkas and MANPADS.
  • Target coordinates that Overlord reads out also appear as a mark on the requesting flight's F10 map.
