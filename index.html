Your DCS server, on the radio.

Buddy Radio puts AI controllers on your group's SRS frequencies. Ask Overlord for a picture, get taxi from the tower, learn the carrier break from Paddles. Every number comes from the live mission.

    Controllers that know the mission

    A small script in the mission sends positions, airfields, weather and carriers to a program on your PC. That program listens on SRS, works out the answer and speaks it back on the frequency.

    The whole flight hears it
    The stations join your SRS server like any other radio. Everyone tuned in hears the call, and each pilot is tracked on their own, by name.
    Real numbers, not guesses
    Bearings, ranges, altitudes, runways, wind and coordinates are calculated from the mission as it runs. The AI only puts them into words.
    Most calls cost nothing
    Bogey dope, taxi, ATIS, threat warnings, talk-downs, coordinates and the whole landing trainer run in code. Claude is used only for free-form questions, which comes to cents per evening.
    Free speech, no menus
    Talk the way you would to a real controller. No F10 menu, no VoiceAttack profile, no fixed phrases to learn.

    How it fits together

    Nothing changes for the pilots: they fly DCS and talk on SRS as usual. The server runs a mission with the Buddy bridge, and one PC runs Radio Ops.

    Speech recognition (Whisper) and voices (Piper) run locally on that PC. Only the free-form questions go to Claude.

    A live map for your tablet

    Radio Ops also serves a map: friendlies and hostiles, SAM rings, airfields with the runway in use, carriers, bullseye, and every target Overlord has read out. Tap anything for bearing and range from your jet.
